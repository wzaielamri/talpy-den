# Features

**See everything at a glance**

- **Panel icon:** Talpy peeking up through the panel's edge, in the state color: orange and popping up, with a red badge counting the chats that need you; blue and glancing around while Claude works; green and smiling when it's your turn; grey and asleep when nothing runs. The 5-hour usage of your default account sits next to it (see [Accounts](usage.md#accounts)).
- **The big Talpy** at the top of the popup sums it all up. Its stage glows orange (gently pulsing) when a chat needs you, blue while chats work, green when it's your turn, and turns into a starry night once everything is done. Its headline says what matters most, e.g. *2 chats need you*: click it to go there. Under it, a line says what the working chats do right now (*fix parser ▸ Edit: parser.py +12 −3*), each new step as it comes, taking turns between the chats at work: click it for that chat's pane (Plasma and VS Code). Double-click its stage a little away from Talpy and there's a cookie for him, now and then of another flavour: he spots it, runs after it as you drag it around (and jumps for it when you hold it up; let go and it floats down to the floor, where you can pick it up again), eats it, tastes it his way, says thanks now and then, and digs in right there. Nothing is counted: a cookie is just a treat (Plasma and VS Code).
- **Changes show the moment they happen:** Talpy Den watches the files Claude Code keeps for its chats, so a chat that starts, ends, starts working, asks for something or finishes its turn shows at once (also when you answer or interrupt it in its terminal), with its pet, notification and sound. **Live mode** (a setting) shows each step of each chat as it happens too: every tool call in its row, its pane and the line under the big Talpy. Between changes Talpy Den checks far less often than before, so it costs less while your chats sit idle. (Not on GNOME yet.)
- **One section per Claude account** (profiles logged into the same account are merged; the default account comes first): plan usage with reset countdowns (from the status line; for chats in Claude's VS Code panel, which runs none, Claude Code fetches it while they work: see [How it works](how-it-works.md)), tokens for today and the last 7 days, and every chat (terminal, background job or VS Code) with its state, how long it's been in it, project folder, model and reasoning effort (e.g. *Opus 5.5 · max*, and *ultracode* when it's on) and context fill, plus what a waiting chat asks for (e.g. *approve Bash: rm -rf build/*).
- **Focus on what needs you:** the filter button in the popup's header (**Focus**) shows only what needs you, as full rows (their questions, Allow and Deny, Go on), the chats that finished a turn you haven't looked at yet (until you open that chat), one line for each chat at work with what it does now, and how the rest stands. **Show everything** goes back to every account, chat, pet and usage bar.
- **What each chat is doing, without switching to it:** under each chat, a line says what it's doing right now (`▸ Bash: npm test`), what it needs, Claude's recap of it (when you come back to a chat after a while, Claude Code writes one: *You asked …; Next step: …*) or what Claude said last, with a bar for its task list (*2/5*). Click the line to read your last prompt, Claude's reply and the chat's tasks. A chat in plan mode, auto mode, accept edits or don't ask says so next to its name; one that bypasses permissions says so in red.
- **Chats a usage limit stopped** say so in red, with when the limit resets (a background chat too, which Claude marks as blocked); their pet runs out of steam, and the notification says the chat hit the usage limit, not that it finished. Once it has reset, a notification says which chats can go on. Meanwhile **Continue on personal** moves the chat to another account with room left (see [Sharing chats](sharing.md)), or **Go on at 10:41** has it go on by itself once the limit resets: in the background, in the permission mode it had and on the model and reasoning effort it last ran on (the answer says which, e.g. *Opus 5.5 · xhigh*), telling Claude the limit has reset. A background chat that's still running takes that in where it is, as `claude agents` answers one: the same chat, its commands running on, and a terminal that shows it (`claude attach`) stays on it. Otherwise an idle Claude that still has it open quits first; one still running commands it started in the background, a server say, is waited for, and ended 15 minutes after the go-on's time, with a notification as it starts waiting and as it ends them. **Keep going** beside it does that after every later limit too, until the chat is done (with no reset to go by, out of usage credits say, it tries again after 2, 5, 15, 30 and 60 minutes, then gives up and says so); a chat at work gets the same from its ↻ button. A chat on an SSH host or in a container can keep going too: it's kept on this computer and goes on from here over ssh, as a go-on you queue there does, once the host's latest check says a limit stopped it (a host that hasn't answered for 15 minutes waits, and its *Keeps going* tag says so). A go-on (or task) queued for a host or a container that couldn't be reached when its time came (Disconnect, no network, a stopped container) starts by itself once it answers again; one the host turned down, or didn't answer in time (it may have started there), waits for you. And one of a host or a container that isn't shown (you unpicked it, or containers are off) is still listed, where the hosts go, with **Show** and **Stop** or **Cancel**: it goes on all the same (Talpy Den still checks that host or container, as often as the ones shown, while something of it keeps going or is queued). A chat that keeps going stays listed until it's done or you turn Keep going off, also once its terminal is closed; and when closing its terminal makes Claude move it to the background under a new id (a copy of its history), Keep going, its go-on, its row (with what's unfolded in it), its pet, its star, its name, a Hide and its open Talpy Chat window follow it there (its row's *Background* tag says when it moved; the Projects tab and search list it once, under the new id), and the old id is never resumed (that made two copies of the conversation work at once). In **Recent chats**, click a chat's **Limit** tag (once) or **↻** (keep going) for the same. Stopping a chat that keeps going (■) asks whether to leave Keep going on (**Stop**: it goes on after the next limit) or to turn it off too (**Stop and turn Keep going off**).
- **Where your usage is heading:** each usage bar shows where you'd be when the window resets at your pace (the faded part), a mark where an even pace would be by now, and a warning when you'd run out first (*At this pace: full in 1h 20m, 40m before it resets*).
- **A usage cap** (a setting, off until you set it): once an account's 5-hour or weekly window reaches the share you pick (say 90%), the account's chats at work on this computer are stopped, once in that window (in tmux and VS Code's Claude the turn is interrupted and the chat stays open). Queued tasks wait until the window resets, and chats that keep going go on then. Chats you start or answer yourself afterwards aren't stopped again (Plasma and VS Code).
- **Your last days in numbers:** click an account's token line for its last 14 days: tokens per day as bars, chats and prompts, your busiest folders and the models used. Each chat's unfolded box ends with its own recap (*3 prompts · worked 54m · 313 tool calls (Bash 265, Read 25) · 100.6M tokens · $37.06 at API prices*).
- **Your days with Claude:** the popup's **Recap** tab shows a day, your last 7 days, your last 30 days or everything since your first chat (**Day | Week | Month | Overall**; step back a day, a week or 30 days at a time; a month has a row per 7 days, as everything does, each saying its days (*23–29 Oct*; the oldest can be shorter: *7–8 Oct*), and compares with the 30 days before). Claude Code deletes chats after 30 days, but the Recap keeps their numbers, so **Overall** keeps growing (a setting turns that off, asking first when the history holds chats Claude has deleted or your plan's windows that ended, since they then go for good; **Delete Saved History…** starts it again). For the 7 days, from the big picture down to the money: how long you were at it, how long Claude worked, your prompts, its tokens and what the same work would cost at Claude's API prices, against the week before (while today isn't over, the days before count up to this time of day, so nothing looks down just because the day isn't done), with a trend line of the last five weeks under each; what Claude did (tool calls, commands, edits with the files and lines they changed, subagents, web searches, compactions, and the other tools it used most); a timeline with a row per day that shows when you were at it, when Claude worked (darker where several chats worked at once) and when it waited for your answer (point at a day to see what went on then; click a day for its chats), red marks where a usage limit stopped a chat; on today's **Day**, how full your plan's windows are now and how they filled up; folded under it, your plan's limits over time; on a **Day**, one timeline chat by chat, the day's own row (*All chats*) on top and each chat's time and cost at its end, in place of the day's row and the chats it worked on most; the projects Claude worked in (click one to open it in the **Projects** tab) and the chats it worked on most; the API prices by model, with what the cache saved; and what would use less of your plan: the tips of Claude Code's own `/usage` (*Start fresh when you switch tasks*, *Save max effort for hard tasks*, *Compact before you step away*, …), each with the share of the usage it touches (*86% of usage*; one request can count for several) and a command to copy, its icon saying where it comes from, and what coming back to chats whose cache had expired cost. For all your accounts or one, in US dollars or your currency. It's read from the chats on this computer and the SSH hosts shown, so it uses no tokens, and only while the tab is open (the first time takes a few seconds; after that only what's new is read). On Mondays a notification sums up the last 7 days (*Your week with Claude*), counting the SSH hosts shown as the Recap does, and opens the Recap (a setting turns it off).
- **The permissions you gave, in one place:** the popup's **Permissions** tab lists everything that lets chats go without asking you, here and on the SSH hosts shown: rules, modes and loosened checks in your settings, your administrator's and every folder's `.claude` settings, approved MCP servers, and the folders you trusted. **Revoke** changes that one entry in its file (on the host for a host's). Under it, the permission prompts you answered in the last 7 days, with the rules that would have let the most of them through (**Allow** writes one, **Undo** takes it back; a setting leaves them out). Read from your settings and the transcripts, so it uses no tokens.
- **A pet for every chat:** Talpy lives in a burrow, and each account's chats get one on a little stage under its name, in the same order as the list (the chats that need you first, the others in the order they started), wearing the chat's name and a calm version of its color. The chat's row in the list gets a bar in that color. A pet acts its chat out, half out of its burrow: it works (types, thinks, hunts bugs, checks tests, juggles subagents), pops up waving or holding up a sign when the chat needs you, plays when it's your turn, sleeps once the chat is done and looks sad if it failed. The lamp on its head glows in the chat's state color. Click a pet to go to its chat (its terminal or VS Code tab).

**Act on your chats**

- **Chat with any chat from the panel:** click a chat and it opens in Talpy Chat, beside the popup, in a window of its own (another chat opens in another window, the first stays open, and clicking a chat whose window is open brings it forward, as its tab does in VS Code): the conversation as it goes, drawn the way Claude's terminal draws it: a dot before each reply and tool call, Claude's replies rendered (headings, lists, code blocks with a **Copy** button, tables; it says *Copied* once it has, and for a command Claude wrote for its prompt, `! chmod …`, it copies the command without the `!`), each tool call on one line with what came of it under it (*● Bash(pytest) ⎿ 3 passed*, the dot green or red; click it for what it ran and got), updating while it works. While Claude works, a line at the end says what it's doing and, at its end, how long it has worked on your message and how many steps it took (*8m 12s · 58 steps*), with **Stop**: what Claude does now stops, as Esc in its terminal, and the chat stays and takes your next message (the conversation says *Interrupted*). Stop is there for a chat in tmux (Ctrl+C in its pane) and in the background (Talpy Den types the Esc through Claude's background service, at the size a terminal attached to it has, so that terminal doesn't change; Claude Code 2.1.318 and later 2.1 versions); elsewhere the line's tooltip says where to stop it. The header's *Context 69% left* says what's left of the chat's context before Claude compacts it on its own, counted as Claude Code counts it, in the warning colour once that's close (its tooltip: the tokens used of its model's window, and `/compact` to choose what it keeps); none when it can't be known (a model Talpy Den doesn't know). A line comes before the first item of each new day (*Today*, *Yesterday*, *Mon 3 Nov*), none before the first; with the pointer on a message, yours or Claude's reply, its time (*14:32*, *Yesterday 14:32*) and **Copy** show over its top right corner (a reply as Markdown, as Claude wrote it, all of it: one Talpy Chat shows cut is read whole first; yours as you wrote it; it says *Copied* once it's on the clipboard). **Keys** for everything: Ctrl+Enter sends now, Alt+Enter when the turn ends, Alt+1…4 a suggested reply, Alt+Y / Alt+A / Alt+N answer what it asks, Ctrl+↑ / Ctrl+↓ go from message to message, Ctrl+Shift+C copies Claude's last reply, Esc closes what's open and then stops Claude, and more: **?** in an empty box lists them, and buttons' tooltips name theirs ([the list](usage.md#talpy-chats-keys)). **Search:** Ctrl+F opens a find bar over the conversation's top right; the matches near what you read are marked, *12 matches*, and the conversation stays where you read until Enter (or F3) goes to the next one from there, Shift+Enter to the one before (*3 of 12*); on the Steps tab it finds what each step ran and got too, and a match in a folded part unfolds it. **Folds:** a message, reply or command output longer than about 40 lines shows its first 24 with *Show more · N more lines* (*Show less* folds it again; a step's long output too); what Talpy Chat first reads folds, but Claude's last reply, and what comes in while you watch doesn't; Ctrl+O unfolds every long one and your answered questions and plans, and again folds them back. Talpy Den's keyboard shortcut can bring up the chat that needs you in Talpy Chat. **Right-click** the conversation for Copy, Copy message and Select all, or the reply box for Undo, Redo, Cut, Copy, Paste (a picture too) and Select all, each with its key. Scrolled up while new messages come in, *2 new ↓* shows over the bottom of the conversation: a click goes to the end. **A−** / **A+** (or Ctrl+wheel) set its text size. Below it, a box to reply (Enter sends, Shift+Enter starts a new line, **Ctrl+V** pastes a picture, shown under the box): **Send**, or **Send when this turn ends** (Talpy Den keeps it and sends it once the turn is over; on an account at its usage limit, once the limit resets). Your message shows at once, dimmed (*Sending…*) until the chat has it. One sent while Claude works waits 5 s first, *Queued · goes to Claude in 5 s*, with **Edit** (back in the box; ↑ in an empty box too), **Send now** and **Cancel**; then Claude takes it in at its next step. ↑ in an empty box also brings back the messages you sent before, one at a time, as in Claude's terminal. **Suggested replies:** once Claude's turn is over, small chips above the box offer a reply in one click (nothing is sent without one): first the answers to the question Claude ended with, found by its shape (its options as Claude numbered them, the one it recommends in bold; the two halves of an either/or; *Yes* and *No* in the question's language), then what you usually reply after a turn that ended the same way (after a usage limit, a chat that stopped…), from your own chats on that account, shown only when it's a good share of what you sent then. A click sends it as if you typed it; Shift+click, or *Edit before sending* on its right-click menu, puts it in the box to add to it; Alt+1…4 picks one; *Don't suggest this* hides one of your usual replies for good. They never show while Claude works or asks, nor once you type, and after five messages in a row that weren't one of them, none for half an hour. What you type is completed in grey from your usual replies (Tab or → takes it). *Suggest replies* in Settings → Chats turns them off (*Suggest Hidden Replies Again* there brings back the ones you hid). **Commands:** a command you ran shows in the chat as yours, wherever you ran it (`/context`, `/model fable`, a skill, `!ls` after a `$`), in the code font, with what it printed under it in Claude's colours. The box's `/` list offers Claude's commands (as of Claude Code 2.1.318), yours and the project's, each where it runs: most run from here (`/compact`, `/context`, `/recap`, `/rename`, `/goal …`, your commands and skills); `/permissions` opens Talpy Den's Permissions tab (*Talpy Den*); one that needs Claude's own panel or a dialog (*Terminal*: `/config`, `/btw`, `/clear`, `/add-dir`) opens in the chat's terminal with the command typed in for you to press Enter there (into its tmux pane; elsewhere it's on the clipboard to paste there), so a background chat never sits in a panel nobody sees. `/model` and `/effort` show Talpy Chat's own list (Opus, Sonnet, Fable, Haiku or Claude's default; the effort levels), which sets them for that chat alone, never as your default as Claude's own would: a background chat open idle stops and wakes again with them in under a second, one that isn't open has them from your next message; in a chat in a terminal they open there, where Claude also makes them your default for every new chat. One that can't run on that chat is greyed, and the line under the box says why: a shell command (`!ls`) in a chat that isn't running or a new one (Claude would read it as a message), a command for Claude's own panel in a new chat (start it first). Your message goes the way the chat can take it: typed into its tmux pane; into the input of its VS Code tab (press Enter there); into a running background chat, as `claude agents` answers one (while it works, it takes it once it can; a terminal that shows it stays on it); or, for a chat that isn't open, the chat goes on with it in the background (`claude --bg --resume`, the same chat). A terminal like Konsole takes no text from outside, so there you choose: send it, and Claude in the terminal quits and the chat goes on in the background, or copy it and open its tab. An SSH host's chats work the same, over the connection Talpy Den already has. A notification takes a reply too.
- **Dictate a reply:** the pane's microphone (**Dictate**) records what you say (up to five minutes, with `pw-record`, `parecord` or `arecord`) and writes it into the reply box, with speech to text that runs on this computer: what you say never leaves it, and the recording is deleted once it's written down. The first click offers to set it up, with no sudo: faster-whisper from pypi.org in a Python environment of its own and its base speech model from huggingface.co (about 600 MB), in `~/.local/share/talpy-den/whisper` (with whisper.cpp installed, that's used instead and only a model is downloaded). The settings pick the language, download, delete or pick other models (up to 1.5 GB each), and **Remove faster-whisper…** deletes it all again.
- **Answer permission prompts from the panel:** a chat that asks for a permission shows what it asks, the command or the file with its change (any other tool, an MCP server's or a page fetch: what it's called with, as `key: value` lines, the first three in a row and a notification, all of them in the pane), with **Allow once**, **Always allow** and **Deny** (with a note for Claude), in Claude's own order, in its row, its pane and its notification (a prompt that takes the place of the one shown, a parallel subagent's say, says *New prompt* and its buttons wait a moment, so a click meant for the one before doesn't answer it). A notification shows a command of more than 3 lines or 200 characters (or arguments that long) only in part, with *… N more lines: Open to read it all*, and offers **Open** (the chat's pane, with all of it), **Allow once** and **Deny** for it; **Always allow** is then in the row and the pane. In a background chat's window, your reply answers what it asks, as typing in its own prompt does, and the chat is never stopped for it: **Deny with this note** (a plan: **Keep planning with this note**), or one question's answer in your own words (several: **Decline with this note**). That takes the opt-in setting *Answer permission prompts from Talpy Den*: it adds a hook to each Claude profile's `settings.json` (those in *Extra config dirs* too, and on the SSH hosts shown) that hands each request to Talpy Den. Claude still asks in the chat too; whichever you answer first counts. Where that changes the chat's mode, the button says what it does instead: **Allow all edits** for a file edit (Claude's own *Yes, allow all edits during this session*: its accept edits mode, in which commands still ask). **Always allow** does what Claude suggests, as its own *don't ask again* does, and its tip (in a notification, a line next to the button: *Always allow: …*) says what that is and where it goes: a rule (`Bash(git push:*) in this project (.claude/settings.local.json)`), access to a folder, or all edits in this chat (accept edits mode). Chats in tmux can be answered without the hook, with their dialog's keys, unless the prompt may be about another call than the one shown (a subagent's, or one of several calls Claude made at once): those you answer in the chat. **Keyboard answers** (a setting, off until you turn it on) answer the chat that has waited longest for a permission from anywhere: **Meta+Alt+Y** allows it once (a plan: approves it), **Meta+Alt+N** denies it (a plan: keeps planning), and a note on screen says what was answered; pick other keys in System Settings → Shortcuts → Talpy Den. In VS Code they're **Talpy Den: Allow the Oldest Prompt** and **Deny the Oldest Prompt** (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Y and Ctrl+Alt+Shift+N). Both take the hook.
- **Answer Claude's questions from the panel:** when Claude asks you a multiple-choice question, its row and its pane show each question with its options (choose one, or any for a question that takes several) and a field to answer in your own words, then **Send answers** or **Decline…** (with a note for Claude). Its notification has a button for each option when it's one question with one answer, then **Other…** (the pane, ready to type); with more questions, **Answer…** opens the pane. Your answers reach Claude as they would from its own dialog. This also takes *Answer permission prompts from Talpy Den* (without it, the question shows, to answer in the chat).
- **Review what a chat did:** the pane's **Review** tab lists the files it changed with their added and removed lines, against how they were before the chat (from Claude's own backups), each with its diff and **Undo**; **Undo all** puts them all back (a file the chat made is deleted for good, and the question says so). **Commit these files…** commits only this chat's files (yours stay as they are, and files git ignores are left out) with a message made from its name and Claude's recap, that you edit first. Finished chats that changed files say **To review** until you **Mark reviewed** (or until they change files again), with whether tests ran and passed after their last edit (**Tests ✓**, **Tests ✗**, **Not tested**).
- **Jump to a chat** with the window button beside it or its pet (or by clicking its name, when the setting *A click on a chat opens it in Talpy Chat* is off): Konsole switches to the right tab (or tmux to its pane), VS Code opens the conversation, and a background job is attached in a terminal. Finished chats reopen with `claude --resume`.
- **Start a new chat** with the speech bubble with a plus next to an account: pick one of its recent project folders, or **Choose folder…** to pick any folder in the folder dialog, and a terminal opens with Claude in it. The **Open in: Talpy Chat | Terminal | Claude Code panel** switch at the top of the list picks where (kept for next time, and for recent chats too): VS Code's Claude Code panel, or **Talpy Chat**, where the chat runs in the background and its window opens on it (see below). Without the `claude` command, **Terminal** and **Talpy Chat** are greyed out; without VS Code, **Claude Code panel** is (the tooltip says why).
- **Open in: Talpy Chat:** a new chat with a task starts in the background at once, and its Talpy Chat window opens on it with your message shown as sent and *Starting…* until Claude takes it; without a task, Talpy Chat opens empty for that account and folder (*New chat in ~/x*), with a ⚙ line above the box for the model, effort and permission mode it starts with, and your first message there starts it. What it asks, permission prompts, Claude's questions and its plan, is answered in Talpy Chat with nothing to turn on: a chat Talpy Chat starts or wakes gets Talpy Den's permission hook for itself alone (`claude --bg --settings`; none when *Answer permission prompts from Talpy Den* has it in every chat already), and nothing of Claude's settings files changes. A recent chat reopens in Talpy Chat, where your next message goes on with it in the background. Whatever Open in says, a background chat no terminal shows has Talpy Chat as its window (its pet, its window button, its notifications; this computer's), and **Open in a terminal** there shows it in one too (`claude attach`). Turning *Answer permission prompts from Talpy Den* off while chats Talpy Chat started answer their prompts with their own hook asks first whether they keep it. A prompt Talpy Chat can't answer (a background chat started elsewhere) has **Answer it in a terminal**. A folder Claude doesn't trust yet starts in a terminal, where Claude asks (its line in the list says so). One you pick with **Choose folder…** is asked about first, in the words of Claude's own question (*Trust It, Open in Talpy Chat* or *Open in a Terminal*): yes is kept where Claude keeps it, the chat opens in Talpy Chat, and the Permissions tab lists the folder with **Revoke**. This computer's chats only for now: on SSH hosts and in containers, and for new chats where an organization's managed settings let only their own hooks run, it's greyed out, and the tooltip says why.
- **Start it with a task, also in the background:** write what Claude should do in the field above the folders, then click a folder (Ctrl+Enter: the first one). In a terminal the task is sent right away, in VS Code it's typed into the new conversation for you to send. With **In the background** no window opens: Claude's background service runs the chat (`claude --bg`), and it's listed right away, so you see when it needs you. That works in folders Claude trusts already (it can't ask there), so never in your home folder. Pick the model, the reasoning effort and the permission mode (manual, accept edits, plan, auto, …) it starts with above the folders; the next chat starts with the same. The field grows with what you write, and **Ctrl+V** pastes a picture from the clipboard (a screenshot, say) for Claude to look at, with a thumbnail under the field (it needs `wl-paste` or `xclip`). For an SSH host's chat, the box asks to copy it to the host first.
- **Wait for the usage limit, or start later:** with an account at its limit, **After the limit resets** queues a background task (in a recent folder, or **Choose folder…**): it starts by itself a minute after the limit resets. **Later:** starts it on a day and at a time you pick instead (today or in the next six days). Queued tasks (and chats that go on by themselves) are listed under their account, to start now or cancel (a task or a reply with your own text in it asks first, since the text goes with it).
- **Two chats changing the same files** get a red **⚠ Same files** tag (and a notification): they can overwrite each other's work.
- **Memories:** each project in the **Projects** tab lists what Claude remembers there: read one, change it (the pencil), hide it (the struck-out eye) or delete it (the trash button). Pointing at one shows its folder, *(not there any more)* when that folder was moved or deleted. Each account has its own `CLAUDE.md` as its last line (your instructions for every chat on it): read it, or change it (✎).
- **Find a chat:** the magnifier at the top looks through all your chats, here and on the SSH hosts shown: every one with all the words you type in its name, its folder or one of your prompts, newest first. Click one to go to it, or to reopen it. It finds project folders (click one for a new chat there), plans and memories with those words too. Each kind, and each computer, folds under its heading (the chats show at first); **Unfold all** shows everything.
- **Plans:** a chat that wrote a plan in plan mode gets a **Plan** tag (with the plan icon) that opens it rendered (in Okular where it's installed, else in your browser; a setting can pick VS Code's preview or your own app), and a pencil tag that opens its Markdown file to change it, in your text editor (an SSH host's in VS Code, over Remote-SSH). Once you changed a plan, **↩ Send to Claude** sends your changes (and notes you wrote into it) to the chat that made it, which revises the plan in plan mode. Every plan is listed in its project (the folder of the chat that made it) in the **Projects** tab, and the ones you starred under their account's **★ Starred plans**: open one, edit it (✎), send your changes (↩), **carry it out** in a new chat (the plan is the chat's first message, with your own notes, in a terminal, VS Code or the background), go back to the chat that made it, share it with your other accounts, hide it (the struck-out eye) or delete it (the trash button). Plans Claude keeps in a project's own folder are listed too: the folder its `plansDirectory` setting names (in your Claude settings, or the project's `.claude/settings.json` or `.claude/settings.local.json`), and its `.claude/plans` (never a plan that's a link, nor a folder that leads out of the project through one); each once, under the account whose chat made it, with no Share button (every account has them there). A plan keeps its project once the chat that made it is deleted, and a project's own plans folder is still listed once Claude has deleted its chats. A file a chat wrote into `plans/` itself (a progress log it keeps there, say) is listed in the project of the chat that wrote it, or of its subagent. For each account, under its plan with its tier (*Pro*, *Max 5×*, *Max 20×*, *Team · standard seat*, …): the size of each window its plan has (also Max's weekly *Opus* and *Sonnet* windows, which count only that model's usage) and how it changed against the week before (a change under 10% is *about the same*); a chart of every 5-hour window (a dot each) with the weeks as bars under them, every account's on the same days so they line up; how much of the week a full 5-hour window is; how long one lasts at your pace (and how long Claude worked in it); and what a full window is in tokens (at the API price of the model you use most, and your own mix). Two plans or more are side by side. When your limits change, a bigger or smaller plan, or Anthropic's own change, the readings show it: the same usage reads as a different share of the window from one moment to the next, with nothing used between. Talpy Den then says when and by how much (*your limits grew about 5× · Pro → Max 5×*), marks it on the chart, measures before and after apart, and until a window is measured in the new limits shows the one before times the change (*≈*). A plan change between two windows, that no reading saw, is told by the plan the windows were on. It's an estimate: use on claude.ai, your phone, other computers or SSH hosts counts toward your limits but isn't seen here, so a limit can look smaller than it is. It reads the limit readings Talpy Den kept (`usage-past.json`, `usage.json`, the status-line copies) and the Recap's own cache, only when you open it.
- **Usage forecast:** your pace is the last hour's in the 5-hour window, and the window's so far in the weekly ones (or while there's no hour on record yet). Talpy Den notes each window's usage as it changes, in `~/.cache/talpy-den/usage.json`. A window that ended stays in `~/.cache/talpy-den/usage-past.json` for 90 days, with the plan it was on and the time it reached each 1% (each 5% in the 5-hour window), to look back at how much your windows held and whether a limit changed. It follows the Recap's *Keep a history beyond Claude's 30 days*: off, none is kept, and **Delete Saved History…** deletes it too. Each SSH host and container you watch keeps its own, of the accounts there, and it follows the same setting at its next check.
- **Your phone (Plasma):** with *Phone* on, once the screen has been locked as long as set, a chat that needs you is sent to your phone with `kdeconnect-cli --ping-msg` (its name, what it asks and its folder), and if you like a chat that finishes a turn too. The phone gets it while KDE Connect reaches it (the same network). Pair the phone in System Settings → KDE Connect first.
- **While you're away:** Talpy Den asks every 10 s whether the screen is locked. Once it has been for 5 minutes (a setting), chats that finish or need you don't notify one by one; when you unlock, one notification (if notifications are on) and a card at the top of the popup say what happened since you left. Clicking the notification goes to the chat that needs you.
- **Notifications:** when a chat needs you (a permission prompt, a question, or a background job that stopped to ask you something; closed again once you answer), and when a chat finishes a turn that took at least 30 seconds (configurable, or off), or a usage limit (or an expired login) stops it. Sounds: one each for "needs input", "finished" and "failed", from your sound theme, a sound file of your own, or none. They go through PipeWire's notification stream, so the system's notification volume applies. When several chats change at once, only the most urgent sound plays.
